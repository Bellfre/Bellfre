You are moving from "code ownership" into protocol governance, and those require different protections.

The important distinction:

Copyright/license protects the implementation
Trademark protects the identity
Terms of use protect the network behavior
Governance rules protect the standard

A license alone will not stop someone from cloning the idea. It can stop unauthorized use of your code, trademarks, specifications, and official ecosystem identity.

Your brain dump is actually describing something closer to an Open Standard Governance License plus a Trademark and Network Participation Agreement.

I would separate this into documents rather than one giant license.

Bellfre Governance Framework (Draft Structure)
1. Software License (SDK / Code)

Purpose:
Protect Bellfre source code while allowing adoption.

Something like:

Open source components may be used according to license
Commercial redistribution requires compliance
No creation of competing Bellfre-branded services
No removal of attribution
No impersonation of official Bellfre infrastructure

Potential models:

Option A — Apache 2.0 + Trademark Agreement

Most developer friendly.

Option B — Business Source License (BSL)

Better if you want a conversion date or commercial restrictions.

Option C — Custom Bellfre Community License

Most control, but more friction.

For what you are describing, I would not immediately use pure MIT. It is too permissive for your stated goals.

2. Bellfre Agent Tag (BAT) Specification License

This is separate.

The BAT specification should become closer to:

RFC documents
ISO-style standards
W3C specifications

The rule:

Anyone may implement the standard, but nobody may redefine the standard.

This protects:

BAT format
validation rules
registry behavior
namespace rules
3. Bellfre Universal Interface (BUI) Governance Rules

This is where most of your concerns live.

Draft principles:

BUI Identity Protection

Any interface representing itself as Bellfre must:

use approved Bellfre branding
accurately identify itself as Bellfre-compatible
follow published BUI requirements
not imply official status without authorization

No unauthorized:

Bellfre portals
Bellfre registries
Bellfre marketplaces
Bellfre gateways
BAT Integrity Rules

A BAT record is immutable once registered.

No participant may:

alter
append
delete
rewrite
reorder
reinterpret

official BAT information.

Corrections must occur through:

new versions
superseding records
governance-approved mechanisms

Never mutation.

BAT Namespace Protection

Only the Bellfre governance authority may issue BAT identifiers.

Participants may not:

create alternate BAT formats
issue unofficial identifiers
mimic BAT formatting
create competing identifiers represented as BATs
Registry Protection

No participant may:

scrape and recreate the registry as a competing Bellfre registry
clone the BUI interface
reproduce Bellfre discovery services
create a substantially similar commercial registry product using Bellfre data
Data Integrity

BAT records may include:

customer Q&A
service descriptions
expertise claims
credentials
pricing information
policies
reviews
verified interaction history
call transcripts where authorized

Rules:

source information must remain attributable
provenance must be preserved
verification status cannot be altered
reviews cannot be modified without proper process
4. Anti-Slop Governance

This is actually a very important concept.

You are describing preventing:

"Anything that technically follows the format but destroys the meaning."

I would formalize this as:

Authenticity Principle

Bellfre implementations must preserve:

accuracy
provenance
trust signals
human/business context

Prohibited:

autogenerated low-quality BAT records
misleading capability claims
artificial reviews
fabricated expertise
unverifiable credentials
5. Governance Committee

Create:

Bellfre Governance Committee (BGC)

Responsibilities:

BAT specification changes
BUI standard updates
version approval
interoperability rules
compliance requirements

Similar model:

IETF RFC process
W3C Working Groups
ISO committees

Rules:

published proposals
review periods
versioned standards
public change history
6. Anti-Clone Clause

This is probably the heart of your concern.

Something like:

The Bellfre standards and ecosystem may be implemented by compatible systems. However, no party may reproduce the Bellfre identity, registry, governance structure, or commercial ecosystem and represent it as an independent equivalent platform.

One thing I would add

You need to avoid accidentally preventing legitimate adoption.

The winning position is:

"You can build on Bellfre."

Not:

"You cannot touch Bellfre."

The valuable asset is not the code. The valuable asset is:

trusted registry
verified BAT history
governance
reputation
network effects

The first mover advantage comes from being the authority, not from hiding the implementation.

bellfre/
└── foundation/
    └── governance/

        ├── BAT_POLICY.md

This actually fits your existing architecture: foundation/spec defines the machine rules; foundation/governance defines the human rules.
